# NYC food trucks and street food vending, for game mechanics

Researched 2026-10-07 for a Big Ambitions mod. Every fact carries a source link. Tags used:

- **[unconfirmed]**: from a single blog, forum, student project or vendor marketing page, or a page I could only read through a search snippet.
- **[inference]**: my own reasoning or arithmetic, not a sourced fact.

Dollar figures are nominal for the year given. Many of the best operational numbers are old (2011 to 2019), because the city has never published a post-pandemic vendor survey ([IBO testimony, May 2025](https://www.ibo.nyc.gov/assets/ibo/downloads/pdf/economy-and-employment/2025/2025-may-street-vendor-testimony-to-the-nyc-council.pdf)).

## Scale of the scene

- About 23,000 street vendors work in NYC: 20,500 mobile food vendors and 2,400 merchandise vendors ([Auerbach 2024, George Mason University](https://jauerbach.github.io/research/auerbach_vendor_2024.pdf); [Immigration Research Initiative, Sept 2024](https://immresearch.org/publications/street-vendors-of-new-york)).
- Roughly 25% of food vendors hold a permit and 75% vend without one. Of 1,400 food vendors surveyed, 349 had a permit ([Auerbach 2024](https://jauerbach.github.io/research/auerbach_vendor_2024.pdf)).
- One third of all vendors work in six ZIP codes: 11368 Corona (16%), 10036 Times Square/Hell's Kitchen (5%), 10019 Midtown West (4%), 10001 Chelsea/Penn Station (3%), 11372 Jackson Heights (3%) and 11354 Flushing (3%) ([Auerbach 2024](https://jauerbach.github.io/research/auerbach_vendor_2024.pdf)).
- Only about 500 of the 5,100 food permits belong to truck operators. The rest are carts ([NYC Food Policy Center](https://www.nycfoodpolicy.org/?p=3633)) [unconfirmed: read via search snippet, page returned 403]. City Journal counted about 100 food trucks citywide in 2015 ([City Journal](https://www.city-journal.org/html/street-food-fight-13741.html)).
- 96% of non-veteran vendors are immigrants. Top origins: Mexico 30%, Ecuador 24%, Egypt 20%, Senegal 7% ([IRI 2024](https://immresearch.org/publications/street-vendors-of-new-york)).

## 1. Permits and licences

### Licence vs. permit

- The **mobile food vendor licence** belongs to a person. It is uncapped, one per person, and requires the Food Protection Course for Mobile Vendors ([DOHMH](https://www.nyc.gov/health/mobilefood)). It costs $50 for two years; honourably discharged veterans pay nothing ([NYC Business](https://nyc-business.nyc.gov/nycbusiness/description/mobile-food-vending-unit-permit-full-term/apply)). The course costs $53 (same source).
- The **mobile food vending permit** belongs to a specific cart or truck, and its number is capped by law ([DOHMH](https://www.nyc.gov/health/mobilefood)). Several licensed people can legally work one permitted unit ([Auerbach 2024](https://jauerbach.github.io/research/auerbach_vendor_2024.pdf)). The permit does not need to be in the vendor's name ([DSNY vendor guide](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dsny/downloads/what-we-do/cleaning/street-vending-enforcement/mobile-food-vendor-educational-highlights-tips.pdf)).

### Permit types and caps

The cap is 5,100 permits ([City Council / NY1](https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/politics/2025/05/07/city-councilmembers-consider-legislation-to-lift-cap-on-vendor-licenses); [Auerbach 2024](https://jauerbach.github.io/research/auerbach_vendor_2024.pdf)):

| Type | Cap | Where and when |
|---|---|---|
| Citywide full-term | 2,800 | All five boroughs, year-round |
| Borough-specific | 200 | One borough other than Manhattan |
| Veterans / disabled citywide | 100 | Citywide, year-round |
| Seasonal | 1,000 | Citywide, April 1 to October 31 |
| Green cart | 1,000 | Whole fruit, vegetables, plain nuts, water; designated precincts only |

Other permits sit outside the cap: **restricted area** permits for private property or Parks land (no waiting list, needs a lease from the owner) and **specialized** disabled-veteran permits limited to park curbs ([DOHMH](https://www.nyc.gov/health/mobilefood); [DSNY vendor guide](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dsny/downloads/what-we-do/cleaning/street-vending-enforcement/mobile-food-vendor-educational-highlights-tips.pdf)).

History: the cap was 3,000 in 1983 and has barely moved since the 1980s ([IBO press release, Jan 2024](https://www.ibo.nyc.ny.us/pressreleases/street-vending-january-2024-pr.pdf)). The green cart law of 2008 split 1,000 permits as Bronx 350, Brooklyn 350, Manhattan 150, Queens 100, Staten Island 50; by August 2011 only 501 were active ([City Limits](https://citylimits.org/study-green-cartsthose-it-could-findare-working/)).

### Fees and renewal

| Item | Fee | Term |
|---|---|---|
| Full-term permit, on-site food prep | $200 initial, $438 renewal | 2 years |
| Full-term permit, pre-packaged food | $75 | 2 years |
| Seasonal permit, on-site prep | $35 | April to October |
| Seasonal permit, pre-packaged | $15 | April to October |
| Licence | $50 | 2 years |

Source: [NYC Business](https://nyc-business.nyc.gov/nycbusiness/description/mobile-food-vending-unit-permit-full-term/apply). Renewal notices go out 90 days before expiry. A new unit must pass a pre-permit inspection within six months or the application lapses. Applicants also need workers' compensation and disability insurance, a sales tax certificate, and no outstanding Health Code fines (same source).

### Supervisory licences (Local Law 18 of 2021, expanded 2026)

- Since July 1, 2022, a new full-term citywide, non-Manhattan or veteran permit requires the applicant to hold a **supervisory licence**. Local Law 18 required 445 supervisory licence applications a year for 10 years ([NYC Council hearing summary](https://citymeetings.nyc/meetings/new-york-city-council/2025-05-06-1000-am-committee-on-consumer-and-worker-protection/chapter/overview-of-local-law-18-of-2021-on-street-vending/)).
- By late April 2025 DOHMH had issued 823 supervisory licences, and those licensees had obtained 382 permits ([DOHMH testimony, May 2025](https://citymeetings.nyc/meetings/new-york-city-council/2025-05-06-1000-am-committee-on-consumer-and-worker-protection/chapter/department-of-health-and-mental-hygiene-testimony-on-street-food-vending-enforcement-and-proposed-legislation)). So fewer than half of supervisory licensees had turned the licence into a working permit [inference from the two numbers].
- A supervisory licensee must be on site: at least one citywide supervisory licensee on a unit working in Manhattan, and one non-Manhattan or citywide licensee outside Manhattan ([DOHMH](https://www.nyc.gov/health/mobilefood)).
- Old permits issued before July 1, 2022 can be renewed without a supervisory licence until July 1, 2032 (same source).
- **2026 expansion.** The Council passed Intro 431-B and overrode Mayor Adams' veto in January 2026 ([NYC Council press release](https://council.nyc.gov/press/2026/01/29/3066/); [PoliticsNY](https://politicsny.com/2026/01/29/city-council-enacts-sweeping-street-vendor-reform-package-after-overriding-last-of-mayor-adams-vetoes/)). From July 1, 2026, DOHMH offers 2,200 new supervisory licences a year for five years, and the city says the new laws (Local Laws 54 and 59 of 2026) add 11,000 mobile food vending permits ([DOHMH](https://www.nyc.gov/site/doh/business/food-operators/mobile-and-temporary-food-vendors.page)). The same package opens 10,500 general (merchandise) vending licences in 2027 ([NYC Council](https://council.nyc.gov/press/2026/01/29/3066/)).

### Waiting list

- Waitlists for food permits and general vendor licences together exceed 20,000 people ([IBO, May 2025](https://www.ibo.nyc.gov/assets/ibo/downloads/pdf/economy-and-employment/2025/2025-may-street-vendor-testimony-to-the-nyc-council.pdf)).
- DOHMH stopped adding names to the food permit list in 2007 when it held 2,000+ names ([WNYC, 2012](https://wnyc.org/story/214757-food-trucks/)). The current lists date from 2022 and new supervisory licences are offered to people on them ([DOHMH](https://www.nyc.gov/site/doh/business/food-operators/mobile-and-temporary-food-vendors.page)).
- Some vendors report waits of up to 20 years ([ABC7 via search](https://abc7ny.com/post/new-york-city-street-food-vendors-speak-permit-scammer-targeting/17467153/)) [unconfirmed].

### Underground permit rental market

Renting or transferring a permit is illegal, but DOHMH acknowledges it happens. Reported two-year lease prices for a $200 permit:

| Year | Price for 2 years | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2011 | ~$14,000 | [WNYC 2012](https://wnyc.org/story/214757-food-trucks/) |
| 2012 | $18,000 | [WNYC 2012](https://wnyc.org/story/214757-food-trucks/) |
| 2015 | $15,000 to $25,000 | [City Journal 2015](https://www.city-journal.org/html/street-food-fight-13741.html) |
| ~2015 | $15,000 to $20,000 | [NYC Food Policy Center](https://www.nycfoodpolicy.org/?p=3633) [unconfirmed, snippet] |
| 2016 | up to $20,000 for a hot dog cart, $30,000 for a truck | [Institute for Justice, 2016](https://ij.org/new-york-city-vendors-rely-on-black-market-to-work/) |
| 2017 to 2019 | ~$25,000 | [6sqft](https://www.6sqft.com/the-state-of-food-trucks-why-owners-are-fed-up-with-outdated-regulations/); [Bedford + Bowery 2019](https://bedfordandbowery.com/2019/03/how-the-food-cart-permitting-black-market-leaves-vendors-scraping-by/) |
| 2022 | $18,000 cash paid by a Lower Manhattan halal cart | [NYT, Feb 2024](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/02/04/nyregion/nyc-costs-chicken-rice-cart.html) |

City Journal reported that 70% to 80% of official permit holders were renting them out ([City Journal](https://www.city-journal.org/html/street-food-fight-13741.html)). Six people were arrested for permit trading in 2009 ([WNYC](https://wnyc.org/story/214757-food-trucks/)). Vendors have also lost money to permit scammers ([ABC7](https://abc7ny.com/post/new-york-city-street-food-vendors-speak-permit-scammer-targeting/17467153/)).

[inference] $18,000 to $25,000 over two years is $9,000 to $12,500 a year, about 45 to 60 times the official fee. Over ~250 vending days that is $36 to $50 a day of "rent" for the right to operate.

### Penalties

DOHMH penalty schedule for mobile food vending ([DOHMH penalty schedule](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/doh/downloads/pdf/rii/mobile-food-vending-unit-penalty-schedule-for-administrative-code.pdf)):

| Violation | Penalty |
|---|---|
| Unlicensed vendor in public space | $1,000 |
| Unpermitted unit in public space | $1,000 |
| Siting violations (12 ft sidewalk clearance, 20 ft from entrances, 10 ft from crosswalk, bus stop, time/place restrictions, display of licence) | Multiple offence schedule: $50, $100, $250, then $500 for each further repeat of the same section within 2 years |
| Selling polystyrene foam containers | $250, $500, $1,000 within 12 months |

- Parking tickets for trucks at meters were $65 each in 2011 ([DNAinfo](https://www.dnainfo.com/new-york/20110822/midtown/food-carts-rule-midtowns-street-lunch-scene-after-trucks-get-boot)). Yumpling in 2019 got up to three tickets on a bad day, $65 to over $100 each, averaging about $600 a month ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/09/20/the-hidden-costs-of-running-a-food-truck.html)). City Journal cited a Midtown truck paying $12,000 a year in parking violations ([City Journal](https://www.city-journal.org/html/street-food-fight-13741.html)).
- In a 2014 NYC Food Truck Association survey, 54% of members had been ticketed the previous day ([Crain's via 34th Street Partnership](https://34thstreet.org/blog/hates-hates-hates-food-carts)).
- **Decriminalisation (2026).** The Council's bill turns unlicensed vending from a misdemeanour (fines $150 to $1,000 and up to three months in jail) into a non-criminal violation with fines up to $1,000, and other time, place and manner violations into civil penalties up to $250 ([6sqft](https://www.6sqft.com/new-legislation-ends-criminal-penalties-for-nyc-street-vendors/); [6sqft on the veto override](https://www.6sqft.com/city-council-overrides-adams-veto-of-street-vending-bill/)).
- Enforcement volume: NYPD issued 9,376 vendor tickets in 2024, five times the 2019 level, and DSNY issued 4,144 ([City Limits](https://citylimits.org/nyc-issued-over-10000-street-vendor-tickets-confiscated-tons-of-food-in-2024/)) [figures from the search snippet; page blocked]. DSNY took over vending enforcement in 2023 with a Vending Bureau of about 40 staff; by November 2024 it had 87 Sanitation Police officers on it (same source).
- The most common civil summons is vending in a prohibited place, followed by no licence or permit. From 2018 to 2022 about 38% of vending civil fines went unpaid ([IBO, May 2025](https://www.ibo.nyc.gov/assets/ibo/downloads/pdf/economy-and-employment/2025/2025-may-street-vendor-testimony-to-the-nyc-council.pdf)).
- A vendor with outstanding fines cannot get a licence or permit (same source).

### Letter grades and inspections

- Carts and trucks have been graded since December 2018 under a 2017 local law. A is 0 to 13 violation points, B is 14 to 27, C is 28 or more ([DOHMH vendor guide](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/doh/downloads/pdf/rii/mfv-what-vendors-need-to-know.pdf)).
- Each unit carries a city location-sharing device so inspectors can find it. DOHMH queries it only when an inspection is due and deletes the data nightly (same source).
- Inspection frequency depends on the last score: A means next inspection in about a year; 14 to 27 points means 5 to 7 months; 28+ means 3 to 5 months (same source).
- A score of 14+ triggers a reinspection within weeks. A B or C on reinspection shows "Grade Pending" until an OATH hearing (same source).

### Commissary requirement

- Every unit must be stored, cleaned and serviced overnight at a DOHMH-approved commissary or depot holding a non-retail food establishment permit, and must return daily. Units selling only whole produce or non-hazardous packaged food can use alternative facilities ([DOHMH](https://www.nyc.gov/health/mobilefood)).
- All food must come from a commissary or another licensed source ([NYC Business](https://nyc-business.nyc.gov/nycbusiness/description/mobile-food-vending-unit-permit-full-term/apply)).
- Commissary capacity is limited, and DOHMH has said any expansion of vending needs more commissary space ([IBO](https://www.ibo.nyc.gov/assets/ibo/downloads/pdf/economy-and-employment/2025/2025-may-street-vendor-testimony-to-the-nyc-council.pdf); [DOHMH testimony](https://citymeetings.nyc/meetings/new-york-city-council/2025-05-06-1000-am-committee-on-consumer-and-worker-protection/chapter/discussion-on-supervisory-license-system-and-the-role-of-commissaries-in-food-vending)).

## 2. Where trucks and carts park, and why

### Legal siting rules

From the city's own vendor guide ([DSNY / DOHMH guide, May 2023](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dsny/downloads/what-we-do/cleaning/street-vending-enforcement/mobile-food-vendor-educational-highlights-tips.pdf)):

- No vending within 10 ft of a crosswalk at an intersection, a subway entrance or exit, or a driveway.
- Carts must stand at the curb, at least 20 ft from any building entrance or exit, and leave a 12 ft clear sidewalk path.
- No vending within 20 ft of a sidewalk café or stoop-line stand.
- No vending in a bus stop, taxi stand, next to a hospital no-standing zone, or on a median that is not a pedestrian plaza.
- Nothing may touch buildings, lampposts, meters, hydrants, benches or trash cans.
- A truck in the roadway must obey all parking rules, and **no vending from a metered parking space** (34 RCNY 4-08(h)(7)).
- The World Trade Center zone (Broadway to West St, Barclay to Liberty) is closed to vending apart from three short exceptions.

**The meter rule.** It dates to 1965 and bans selling merchandise from a metered space. A 2011 court ruling that food counts as merchandise led police to push trucks out of Midtown ([DNAinfo, Aug 2011](https://www.dnainfo.com/new-york/20110819/murray-hill-gramercy/food-truck-crackdown-spreads-midtown-east); [ABA Journal](https://www.abajournal.com/news/article/archaic_law_being_used_to_roust_nyc_food_trucks)). NYC Food Truck Association members (31 at the time) reported sales falls of 20% to 70%, and relocating could take up to two hours, missing the lunch peak ([DNAinfo](https://www.dnainfo.com/new-york/20110819/murray-hill-gramercy/food-truck-crackdown-spreads-midtown-east)). Carts sit on the sidewalk and escape this rule, so some truck businesses (Wafels & Dinges, Desi Food Truck) switched to $15,000 to $20,000 carts ([DNAinfo, July 2011](https://www.dnainfo.com/new-york/20110706/midtown/food-trucks-shifting-strategies-as-police-crackdown-continues); [DNAinfo, Aug 2011](https://www.dnainfo.com/new-york/20110822/midtown/food-carts-rule-midtowns-street-lunch-scene-after-trucks-get-boot)).

**Restricted streets.** DOHMH publishes a list of street segments closed to food vending on set days and hours ([DOHMH restricted streets guide](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/doh/downloads/pdf/permit/mfv_restricted_streets.pdf)). I counted 190 segments: 131 in Manhattan, 44 in Brooklyn, 14 in Queens, 1 in the Bronx. Examples:

- Sixth Avenue, W 33rd to W 59th: closed Mon to Sat 8 a.m. to 7 p.m.
- Lexington Avenue, E 40th to E 57th: closed Mon to Sat 8 a.m. to 7 p.m.
- Madison Avenue, E 46th to E 59th: closed Mon to Sat 10 a.m. to 7 p.m.
- Broadway, W 32nd to W 52nd: closed daily 8 a.m. to 11 p.m.
- Seventh Avenue, W 35th to W 45th: closed Mon to Sat 8 a.m. to 11:59 p.m.
- Financial District streets (Broad, Exchange Place, Maiden Lane, Cedar, Rector): closed weekdays 8 a.m. to 6 p.m.
- Fulton, Liberty, Church (south of Barclay), Greenwich near WTC: closed 24 hours.
- Fifth Avenue 47th to 58th: closed Nov 1 to Jan 15, 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. (holiday shopping).
- Brooklyn: Dyker Heights blocks around 81st to 86th St close for the holiday lights season.

[inference] The restrictions bite hardest exactly where weekday lunch demand is highest: the Midtown avenues and FiDi side streets during office hours. Legal vending there concentrates on the cross streets that are not on the list, which is why blocks like W 49th to W 52nd between Sixth and Seventh became truck rows.

**Parks.** Vending inside a park needs a Parks concession, awarded by auction for five years with fixed annual fees. In 2011 minimum bids for Central Park cart spots ran from $7,350 to $176,925 a year, with a 5% annual increase, and about 40 carts worked in and around the park ([DNAinfo, 2011](https://www.dnainfo.com/new-york/20110405/manhattan/hot-dog-vendors-pay-top-dollar-central-park)). The top spot, Fifth Ave and E 62nd by the zoo, reportedly went for $289,500 a year ([Reddit thread quoting the NYT](https://lr.ggtyler.dev/r/Damnthatsinteresting/comments/1e711q3/permit_for_this_hot_dog_cart_289500_a_year/ldx9cbe)) [unconfirmed]. Parks ran about 400 concessions in 2024, many of them food ([Auerbach 2024](https://jauerbach.github.io/research/auerbach_vendor_2024.pdf)).

### Hot spots in practice

- **Midtown office lunch.** Yumpling parked on W 49th between Sixth and Seventh on Tuesdays and Thursdays. By 7 a.m. four to five other trucks were on the block, which is popular because of the surrounding office towers ([CNBC, 2019](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/09/11/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-new-york-city-food-truck-owner.html)).
- **Penn Station / Herald Square.** On one November evening in 2014, 54 vendors (49 selling food) stood between W 31st and W 35th, Eighth to Fifth Avenue ([Crain's via 34th Street Partnership](https://34thstreet.org/blog/hates-hates-hates-food-carts)).
- **Queens immigrant corridors.** Corona's 11368 alone holds 16% of all vendors ([Auerbach 2024](https://jauerbach.github.io/research/auerbach_vendor_2024.pdf)).
- **Markets.** Smorgasburg runs Saturdays in Williamsburg, Sundays in Prospect Park, and Thursday/Friday at WTC, first weekend of April to last weekend of October, 11 a.m. to 6 p.m.; the 2025 roster had 74 vendors ([Secret NYC via search](https://secretnyc.co/pt/smorgasburg-2025-novos-fornecedores-de-alimentos/)) [unconfirmed]. An older report gave 100 vendors, stall fees of $120 to $225 a day, and 20,000 to 30,000 visitors a weekend ([search snippet of Smorgasburg coverage](https://nyunews.com/2014/04/14/smorgasburg-2/)) [unconfirmed, about 2014].
- **Late night.** The original Halal Guys cart at 53rd and Sixth runs a night cart from about 8 p.m. to 4 a.m., with lines longer after midnight ([SBS Food](https://www.sbs.com.au/food/article/global-roaming-new-yorks-supper-club/9643xaena)).
- **Events and stadiums.** A truck owner at a stadium arrives four hours before game time and stays about three hours after; one lost 90% of his business when a nearby lot closed ([Institute for Justice, Upwardly Mobile](https://ij.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/upwardly-mobile-web-final.pdf)) [Atlanta example, not NYC].

### Rotation, schedules and spot-holding

- Mobile vendors in large US cities operate in three locations on a typical weekday, spend about six hours at their main one, and add two more locations on weekends or event days. 70% pick locations to reach a critical mass of people; only 4% say city rules decide their location ([IJ, Upwardly Mobile](https://ij.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/upwardly-mobile-web-final.pdf)).
- Gourmet trucks run a weekly rotation. Yumpling: Bryant Park Monday, Midtown Tuesday and Thursday, Dumbo Wednesday, SoHo Friday. "It's important ... to stick to a weekly schedule and park in the same places because it helps build and retain a customer base" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/09/11/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-new-york-city-food-truck-owner.html)).
- **Overnight spot claiming.** Most trucks park between 3 and 5 a.m. Yumpling arrived at 1:30 a.m. and an owner slept in the driver's seat, because NYC rules require someone in the vehicle at all times (same source).
- Twitter was a large factor in where trucks go, and truck locations are "spiky"; trucks use mobility to give customers variety ([Anenberg and Kung, J. Urban Economics 2015](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/juecon/v90y2015icp60-78.html); [summary](https://mobile-cuisine.com/off-the-wire/study-on-food-truck-locations-released/)). The data are from Washington DC and national sources, not NYC.
- During the 2011 crackdown some trucks cut social media posts to "stay under the radar", shifted to dinner hours, and leaned on corporate events ([DNAinfo](https://www.dnainfo.com/new-york/20110706/midtown/food-trucks-shifting-strategies-as-police-crackdown-continues)).
- Dual service windows let a truck park facing either way, which "allows you to park in more spots" because some streets only allow serving from one side ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/09/11/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-new-york-city-food-truck-owner.html)).

### Turf

- A Fordham student's field study of Midtown, FiDi and the Upper West Side found an unwritten rule that a truck that claims a spot keeps it; some have held the same spot for over 20 years, and newcomers must find unclaimed ground ([Fordham Ram](https://thefordhamram.com/news/junior-studies-the-dynamics-of-food-truck-positioning/)) [unconfirmed, student research].
- Building owners and BIDs push back. Police began barring trucks from W 51st and W 52nd between Sixth and Seventh about two weeks after building owners complained ([search summary of DNAinfo / Mobile Cuisine](https://mobile-cuisine.com/off-the-wire/nyc-bans-food-trucks-from-yet-another-midtown-location/)) [unconfirmed]. The Grand Central Partnership logged 321 vendor incidents in one fiscal year ([Crain's via 34th Street Partnership](https://34thstreet.org/blog/hates-hates-hates-food-carts)).

## 3. Demand patterns

### Time of day

- Lunch: Yumpling's line formed from 11 a.m.; the window ran 11:30 a.m. to 2:30 p.m. with a queue "consistently dozens of customers deep" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/09/11/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-new-york-city-food-truck-owner.html)).
- Breakfast carts sell egg sandwiches, bagels, coffee, tea and doughnuts from about 5 a.m. to noon; commissaries supply carts with about 1,500 dozen bagels a day ([Business Insider via search](https://kompas.wasmer.app/https_www_businessinsider_com/inside-the-doughnut-factory-where-nycs-food-carts-get-ingredients-2025-11)) [unconfirmed, snippet only].
- Late night: see Halal Guys above. Some halal carts serve breakfast, lunch and early dinner ([CUNY Macaulay student project](https://openlab.macaulay.cuny.edu/nyfoodatlas/?p=704)) [unconfirmed].
- Full-time vendors work 11 to 12 hours a day, 5.5 days a week: about 7 hours serving, 3 hours preparing, 1 to 2 hours on business tasks ([IJ, Upwardly Mobile](https://ij.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/upwardly-mobile-web-final.pdf)).

### Throughput and ticket size

- Yumpling served 200 to 250 customers in a three-hour lunch window and sold 1,200 to 1,400 dumplings, with six staff on board ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/09/12/the-biggest-misconception-of-running-a-food-truck.html)). [inference] That is 67 to 83 customers an hour, or 11 to 14 customers per staff-hour, and about 5 to 6 dumplings per customer.
- Some items are space-capped: Yumpling could make only 15 beef bowls a day and they sold out in 20 minutes, once in 5 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/09/11/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-new-york-city-food-truck-owner.html)). The truck was sold out of almost everything after three hours (same source).
- Prices: chicken over rice at a Lower Manhattan cart was $10 in 2024, up from $6 before the pandemic ([NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/02/04/nyregion/nyc-costs-chicken-rice-cart.html)). A Mister Softee cone is $5, up from $1 ([CNN via KTVZ, 2024](https://ktvz.com/money/cnn-business-consumer/2024/08/24/its-never-been-harder-to-be-mister-softee/)). Central Park carts in 2011 had a city-capped $2 hot dog and $1.25 soda ([DNAinfo](https://www.dnainfo.com/new-york/20110405/manhattan/hot-dog-vendors-pay-top-dollar-central-park)). A breakfast sandwich plus iced coffee is about $9 ([search snippet](https://annekadet.substack.com/p/donut)) [unconfirmed].
- US-wide food truck average ticket of $12 to $15 ([MenuTiger blog](https://www.menutiger.com/blog/average-food-truck-income)) [unconfirmed, vendor blog].

### Weather and season

- Mobile food vendors' profits are fairly stable across seasons: most earn $250 to $1,000 a week in both summer and winter, with more falling under $250 in winter ([IRI survey report](https://immresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/Street-Vendor-Survey-Final.pdf)). Share of permitted food vendors earning over $500 a week: 62% in summer, about 46% in winter; for unpermitted food vendors, 43% and about 33% (same source; winter figures read off a chart).
- 48% of US vendors work full time year-round, 20% full time seasonally, 20% part time seasonally, 13% part time year-round ([IJ](https://ij.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/upwardly-mobile-web-final.pdf)).
- Seasonal permits cover April 1 to October 31 ([DOHMH](https://www.nyc.gov/health/mobilefood)). Mister Softee franchisees now run from early spring through November, longer than before ([CNN via KTVZ](https://ktvz.com/money/cnn-business-consumer/2024/08/24/its-never-been-harder-to-be-mister-softee/)).
- Rain and cold, US anecdotes: lunch business halves in rain; owners report 50%+ drops in December to February; 80°F days sell about 20% more than 60°F days; extreme heat cut one taco truck's sales 30% ([search summary of local news](https://spectrumnews1.com/oh/columbus/news/2026/04/04/weather-bites-food-truck-sales); [Texas Monthly](https://www.texasmonthly.com/food/summer-heat-taco-truck-sales-slump/)) [unconfirmed, not NYC]. Yumpling's line forms "rain or shine" ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/09/12/the-biggest-misconception-of-running-a-food-truck.html)).

### Events and catering

- Many operators make 30% or more of revenue from private events such as weddings and bar mitzvahs of 100 to 200 guests ([QSR Magazine, quoting NYFTA founder Ben Goldberg](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/growth/12-mobile-food-trends-future)).
- The New York Food Truck Association catered 500+ events in 2022 ([NYFTA](https://nyfta.org/a-brief-history-of-the-new-york-food-truck-association)).
- Some gourmet trucks earn as much as half their income from catering and rentals ([search summary of Mobile Cuisine](https://mobile-cuisine.com/?p=2373)) [unconfirmed].

## 4. Economics

### Startup

- Food truck: $100,000 to $150,000 all-in. Used truck bodies ranged from a few thousand dollars to $50,000 to $60,000; equipment is the other big variable ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/09/11/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-new-york-city-food-truck-owner.html)). A small-to-medium restaurant in the city needs about $500,000 by the same owners' estimate ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/09/20/the-hidden-costs-of-running-a-food-truck.html)).
- Food cart: $15,000 to $20,000 ([DNAinfo, 2011](https://www.dnainfo.com/new-york/20110706/midtown/food-trucks-shifting-strategies-as-police-crackdown-continues)). Sammy's Halal's first cart cost $15,000 ([Edible Queens](https://ediblequeens.ediblecommunities.com/eat/sammys-halal-food-carts-jackson-heights-vendy-award)).
- Mister Softee: a franchisee paid $90,000 for a truck ([search summary](https://abc7ny.com/post/new-york-city-summer-staple-melting-mister-softee-ice-cream-trucks-are-slowly-disappearing-streets-nyc-nationwide/15229269/)) [unconfirmed].
- Plus the permit: $200 officially, $15,000 to $25,000 on the rental market (see above).

### Running costs (Yumpling, NYC, 2019)

From [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/09/20/the-hidden-costs-of-running-a-food-truck.html):

| Parking tickets | ~$600 / month average; "a few hundred to a few thousand" |

- Cart storage in a garage/commissary: $250 to $300 a month ([Untapped Cities](https://untappedcities.com/2018/02/12/cities-101-where-do-nyc-food-trucks-go-to-sleep-at-night/)).
- Mister Softee owner insurance: "half a million dollars", likely for a fleet ([CNN via KTVZ](https://ktvz.com/money/cnn-business-consumer/2024/08/24/its-never-been-harder-to-be-mister-softee/)). Wholesale ice cream up 21% and cones up 25% since 2020 (same source).
- Halal cart input costs 2020 to 2024: a 10 lb bag of chicken $22.50 (nearly double), a box of clamshells $28 (from $11), white sauce $13 a gallon (from $9), red sauce $23 a gallon (from $11) ([NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/02/04/nyregion/nyc-costs-chicken-rice-cart.html)).

### Revenue and profit

- **Halal cart, 2024:** on a $3,000 day the Lower Manhattan co-owner pockets about $200 after food, drinks, utensils, propane, maintenance, insurance and wages ([NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/02/04/nyregion/nyc-costs-chicken-rice-cart.html)). [inference] That is a margin of about 7%.
- **Carts after competition:** a vendor whose cart did $2,500 a day did $750 a day across two carts after competitors arrived; marginal-area vendors make about $100 a day; hired cart workers get about $20 an hour or $30 to $40 a day from takings ([City Journal 2015](https://www.city-journal.org/html/street-food-fight-13741.html)).
- **Halal cart owners** in a student interview project reported $200 to $300 gross sales a day ([CUNY Macaulay](https://eportfolios.macaulay.cuny.edu/halalcarts/?p=316)) [unconfirmed].
- **Mister Softee franchisee:** $400 to $900 a day; a normal summer day $200 to $500 gross ([search summary](https://mobile-cuisine.com/franchise/mister-softee/)) [unconfirmed]. The CNN owner sells "half as much ice cream on most days" as he used to ([CNN via KTVZ](https://ktvz.com/money/cnn-business-consumer/2024/08/24/its-never-been-harder-to-be-mister-softee/)).
- **Average vendor profit:** IBO puts average annual profit for mobile food vendors at about $46,000 (2019 survey, 2023 dollars), versus $35,000 for merchandise vendors ([IBO, May 2025](https://www.ibo.nyc.gov/assets/ibo/downloads/pdf/economy-and-employment/2025/2025-may-street-vendor-testimony-to-the-nyc-council.pdf)). The original Jan 2024 IBO report said about $9,000 and was corrected in April 2025 (same source; [IBO press release](https://www.ibo.nyc.ny.us/pressreleases/street-vending-january-2024-pr.pdf)).
- **Weekly profit:** typical food vendor $250 to $1,000 a week ([IRI](https://immresearch.org/publications/street-vendors-of-new-york)). Some cart vendors take home $400 to $500 for a six-day week ([search snippet of 2024 coverage](https://rentwirenyc.substack.com/p/food-cart-owners-strain-to-make-ends)) [unconfirmed].
- **Permit makes a difference:** 62% of permitted food vendors clear $500 a week in summer vs 43% of unpermitted ones ([IRI](https://immresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/Street-Vendor-Survey-Final.pdf)).
- Early days can be thin: Yumpling once served "a whopping total of seven customers" in an afternoon ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/09/20/the-hidden-costs-of-running-a-food-truck.html)).
- US averages, for scale only: about $346,000 annual revenue, net margin 6% to 9%; food cost 25% to 30%; "$500/day to survive, $1,000 comfortable, $2,000 thriving" ([Beancount blog](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/07/19/food-truck-bookkeeping-food-cost-commissary-cash-drops-guide); [MenuTiger](https://www.menutiger.com/blog/average-food-truck-income)) [unconfirmed, vendor blogs].

### Staffing

- Yumpling: three co-founders; a manager starts prep at the commissary, two more staff arrive 9 to 10 a.m., and six people work lunch: fryer, dumplings, two on assembly, register, and one floater ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/09/11/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-new-york-city-food-truck-owner.html)).
- 39% of vendors employ others, averaging 2.3 full-time and 2.7 part-time workers; the median is one of each ([IJ](https://ij.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/upwardly-mobile-web-final.pdf)).
- Carts typically run with one or two people [inference from the 12 ft sidewalk footprint and cart size; no source counts this].

### Failure rates

- Square data put food truck failure at about 25% over three years, versus about 60% for restaurants ([Toast blog citing Square](https://pos.toasttab.com/blog/on-the-line/food-truck-failure-rate)) [unconfirmed, I could not reach the Square original].
- NYC-specific closures: City Journal and Mobile Cuisine report trucks closing over black-market permit costs ([Mobile Cuisine](https://mobile-cuisine.com/off-the-wire/black-market-permitting-closes-another-nyc-food-truck/)) [unconfirmed]. Mister Softee fell from 2,000+ trucks in 38 states in the 1960s to about 630 in 21 states ([CNN via KTVZ](https://ktvz.com/money/cnn-business-consumer/2024/08/24/its-never-been-harder-to-be-mister-softee/)).

## 5. Menu

- Truck kitchens force trade-offs. Yumpling had eight burners and used seven for dumplings; it gave up the driver's door for a second service window, losing space for cooking equipment ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/09/11/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-new-york-city-food-truck-owner.html)). Its menu centred on dumplings, beef bowls and fried chicken sandwiches (same source).
- Industry guidance: small menus speed ordering and cooking, cut stock and keep ingredients fresh, and trucks tend to be known for one thing ([Food Network](https://www.foodnetwork.com/fn-dish/restaurants/things-food-truck-owners-wish-you-knew); [Burkett](https://www.burkett.com/blogs/resources/food-truck-menu-ideas-to-maximize-profitability)) [unconfirmed, industry advice]. No source I found counts NYC menu sizes.
- Carts may store items for sale only in the space directly beneath the unit ([DSNY guide](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dsny/downloads/what-we-do/cleaning/street-vending-enforcement/mobile-food-vendor-educational-highlights-tips.pdf)).

Common NYC categories:

- **Halal carts.** Chicken over rice, gyro/lamb, falafel; Middle Eastern and North African vendors are 22% of all vendors and Egypt is the third origin country ([IRI](https://immresearch.org/publications/street-vendors-of-new-york)). Halal Guys started as a hot dog cart in 1990 ([SBS Food](https://www.sbs.com.au/food/article/global-roaming-new-yorks-supper-club/9643xaena)).
- **Hot dog / pretzel / soda carts.** The Central Park staple ([DNAinfo](https://www.dnainfo.com/new-york/20110405/manhattan/hot-dog-vendors-pay-top-dollar-central-park)).
- **Breakfast/coffee carts.** Coffee, bagels, egg sandwiches, doughnuts, 5 a.m. to noon (see above) [unconfirmed].
- **Ice cream trucks.** Ice cream is the most common food sold by food trucks ([NYC Food Policy Center](https://www.nycfoodpolicy.org/?p=3633)) [unconfirmed, snippet].
- **Tacos, tamales, churros, fruit.** Mexico and Ecuador supply 54% of vendors ([IRI](https://immresearch.org/publications/street-vendors-of-new-york)).
- **Green carts.** Whole produce only ([DOHMH](https://www.nyc.gov/health/mobilefood)).
- **Gourmet trucks.** Korean BBQ (Korilla), Belgian waffles (Wafels & Dinges), Taiwanese (Yumpling), chopped cheese (Bodega Truck) ([WNYC](https://wnyc.org/story/214757-food-trucks/); [Viewing NYC / Eater](https://cityhall.viewing.nyc/video-why-the-best-chopped-cheese-in-nyc-comes-from-a-food-truck/)).

## 6. Competition

- **Clustering and splitting.** One cart's sales fell from $2,500 to $750 a day (across two carts) after competitors set up nearby ([City Journal](https://www.city-journal.org/html/street-food-fight-13741.html)). On the other side, vendor arrivals fit a preferential-attachment model, where new vendors go where vendors already are ([Auerbach 2024](https://jauerbach.github.io/research/auerbach_vendor_2024.pdf)), and Anenberg and Kung find trucks use mobility to supply variety rather than duplicate each other ([J. Urban Economics](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/juecon/v90y2015icp60-78.html)). [inference] Clusters draw more people, but same-cuisine neighbours split them.
- **Truck lots.** Operators like lots because they avoid police, tickets and the 2 a.m. race for a spot; few exist in Manhattan because of land costs ([QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/growth/12-mobile-food-trends-future)). Yumpling served one day a week from a paid private lot to avoid tickets ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/09/20/the-hidden-costs-of-running-a-food-truck.html)).
- **Brick-and-mortar rivalry.** Restaurants and BIDs complain that vendors undercut them without paying rent or café fees; the 34th Street Partnership's president called carts eyesores ([Crain's via 34th Street Partnership](https://34thstreet.org/blog/hates-hates-hates-food-carts); [City Journal](https://www.city-journal.org/html/street-food-fight-13741.html)). Truck operators said local restaurants drove the 2011 crackdown ([DNAinfo](https://www.dnainfo.com/new-york/20110819/murray-hill-gramercy/food-truck-crackdown-spreads-midtown-east)).
- **Ice cream turf.** Mister Softee franchisees get a designated neighbourhood but no protection from outsiders ([CNN via KTVZ](https://ktvz.com/money/cnn-business-consumer/2024/08/24/its-never-been-harder-to-be-mister-softee/)). A former franchisee who had run 16 trucks in Queens and the Bronx kept operating as "Master Softee" and was barred by a federal judge in 2015 ([Law360](https://law360.com/articles/730962)). In 2016 New York Ice Cream drivers, ex-Softee drivers, admitted sending several trucks to stop Softee trucks selling; Softee drivers reported broken windows ([Fox 5](https://www.fox5ny.com/news/ice-cream-truck-turf-war)). Mister Softee also sued a rival in 2015 for copying its jingle ([NY1](https://ny1.com/nyc/queens/news/2015/08/19/mister-softee-takes-hard-line-against-competitor-using-its-jingle-to-sell-ice-cream)).
- **Jingles.** The city noise code allows vending-truck jingles only while the truck is moving ([Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/2016/07/26/ny-ice-cream-truck-jingle-jangles-many-nerves); [Smithsonian](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/arts-culture/making-noise-and-selling-ice-cream-169918723/)).

## Translating to game mechanics

All of this section is **proposals**. Numbers are derived from the facts above and rounded for play; each line names its basis.

### Permits and licences

1. **Two-layer licensing.** A per-employee "vendor licence" ($50, 2 years, a one-day course) and a per-vehicle "permit". Basis: DOHMH licence vs permit.
2. **Permit tiers.**
   - Citywide year-round: official $200 / 2 years, but capped, so a waiting list.
   - Non-Manhattan: same fee, excludes Manhattan, easier to get.
   - Seasonal: $35, valid April to October only.
   - Restricted area: no cap, but only on private lots and events, and each spot needs a lease.
3. **Scarcity via waiting list.** The player joins a list and waits 6 to 24 in-game months for a citywide permit. Shorten it after a scripted "2026 reform" event, mirroring the jump from 445 to 2,200 supervisory licences a year (about 5x). Basis: LL18 and Intro 431-B.
4. **Grey-market permit rental.** Available immediately for $9,000 to $12,500 a year (≈$18,000 to $25,000 per two years), with a 2% to 5% chance per year of the deal collapsing (seizure, scam, or the holder refusing to renew) and the rental fee lost. Basis: reported rental prices; scam and arrest reports. Optionally hide this behind a reputation threshold.
5. **Supervisory licence requirement.** A new citywide permit requires one employee with a supervisory skill on board whenever the truck operates in Manhattan. Basis: DOHMH staffing rule.

### Fines and inspections

6. **Siting fines** on an escalating ladder per rule broken within two in-game years: $50, $100, $250, $500. Basis: multiple offence schedule.
7. **Operating without a permit or licence**: $1,000 per ticket, plus a 10% to 20% chance of stock confiscation. Basis: $1,000 penalty; DSNY confiscations.
8. **Meter tickets for trucks**: $65 to $115 per ticket when parked at a meter, with roughly 1 ticket expected per 2 to 3 Midtown street days (≈$600 a month for a five-day truck). Basis: Yumpling's $600/month average.
9. **Health grade A/B/C.** Grade from hidden cleanliness points: A ≤13, B 14 to 27, C 28+. Inspection interval 12 months for A, 6 for B, 4 for C. Demand multiplier: A 1.00, B 0.90, C 0.75 [inference, no NYC data on grade sensitivity for carts].

### Pitches and location

10. **Pitch quality factor** per street spot, 0.3 to 2.0. Office-tower cross streets in Midtown and FiDi score highest at lunch; Corona/Jackson Heights/Flushing score well all day; Smorgasburg-style markets score highest at weekends. Basis: Auerbach ZIP concentration; Yumpling's W 49th; Smorgasburg schedule.
11. **Restricted street schedule.** Copy the shape of the DOHMH list: Midtown avenues closed 8 a.m. to 7 p.m. on weekdays, FiDi side streets 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. on weekdays, WTC area closed all day. Vending there during those hours triggers the siting fine ladder.
12. **Trucks vs carts**:
    - Cart: $15,000 to $20,000, may use sidewalk pitches, ignores the meter rule, 2 staff max, storage $275/month.
    - Truck: $100,000 to $150,000, needs a legal non-metered curb spot, up to 6 staff, commissary $1,250/month, fuel and propane $600/month, insurance $400/month.
13. **Overnight spot claim.** Prime truck spots are first come, first served. The player can assign an employee to "hold the spot" overnight (a night shift cost) for a 90% chance of getting it; arriving at 7 a.m. gives 20% to 40% on a busy block. Basis: trucks park 3 to 5 a.m., Yumpling at 1:30 a.m., someone must stay in the vehicle.
14. **Weekly route.** Each truck gets a five-day route. Repeat visits to the same spot on the same weekday build a "regulars" bonus of up to +30% over 4 to 8 weeks; skipping a scheduled day costs some of it. Basis: Yumpling's comment on retaining customers [the percentage is invented].
15. **Event and catering contracts.** Bookable gigs with a fixed fee for 100 to 200 guests, with a target share of 30% of revenue for a mature gourmet truck. Basis: NYFTA / Goldberg.
16. **Parks concession auction.** A five-year spot with an annual fee of $7,000 to $180,000 (more for zoo-gate-class spots), rising 5% a year, with very high foot traffic. Basis: Central Park bids 2011.

### Demand

17. **Hourly demand multipliers** for an office-district pitch on a weekday (1.0 = average open hour):

| Hour | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| 6 to 9 | 0.4 (coffee/breakfast carts 1.5) |
| 9 to 11 | 0.3 |
| 11 to 11:30 | 1.0 (line forms) |
| 11:30 to 14:30 | 3.0 |
| 14:30 to 17 | 0.4 |
| 17 to 20 | 0.6 |
| 20 to 4 | 0.2 in office districts; 1.2 at nightlife pitches (halal) |

Basis: Yumpling's 11:30 to 2:30 window and sell-out, breakfast carts 5 a.m. to noon, Halal Guys night cart 8 p.m. to 4 a.m. The exact multipliers are my proposal.

18. **Day of week.** Office pitches: Mon 0.9, Tue to Thu 1.0, Fri 0.85, weekend 0.2. Market/park pitches: weekday 0.4, Sat 1.5, Sun 1.3 [inference, no NYC data found].
19. **Season.** April to October 1.0; November and March 0.8; December to February 0.6 for food trucks and 0.2 for ice cream. Basis: seasonal permit window; IRI shows more food vendors under $250/week in winter and the >$500/week share falling from 62% to about 46%; US anecdotes of 50%+ winter drops.
20. **Weather.** Rain 0.5 at lunch; below 0°C 0.7; above 32°C 0.8 (ice cream 1.5); a mild sunny day 1.1 to 1.2. Basis: US anecdotes [unconfirmed].

### Service and stock

21. **Service rate.** About 12 customers per staff-hour, capped by unit: a truck with 6 staff reaches ~75 customers an hour; a cart with 2 staff ~30 [cart figure is inference]. Basis: Yumpling 200 to 250 customers in 3 hours with 6 staff.
22. **Queue patience.** Customers leave if the line exceeds about 20 to 30 minutes, except for a "destination" truck with high reputation [inference; Halal Guys and Yumpling lines show some customers wait much longer].
23. **Stock limits and sell-outs.** Each unit has a fixed number of storage slots (cart 3 to 4 menu items, truck 4 to 8). Signature items can have a daily cap (Yumpling's 15 beef bowls). A sell-out ends sales of that item for the day and adds a small reputation gain ("sold out" buzz) [the buzz effect is my proposal].
24. **Prices and ticket.** Halal platter $10, hot dog $3 to $4, ice cream cone $5, gourmet truck meal $12 to $15. Food cost 25% to 35% of price; card fees 3%. Basis: NYT, CNN, blogs.

### Economics check

25. **Target outcomes.** A busy Midtown truck should gross about $2,500 to $3,500 on a good weekday (≈220 customers × $12 to $15) and net 5% to 10% after all costs, matching the NYT halal cart ($3,000 gross, $200 net) and the IBO average of about $46,000 a year profit per food vendor. A cart in a marginal spot should gross $100 to $300 a day. If the mod's food truck clears much more than ~$80,000 a year per unit, it is out of line with every source.

### Competition

26. **Same-cuisine splitting, mixed-cuisine pull.** Each neighbouring unit within one block adds +10% to the pitch's total foot traffic, but units selling the same category split that category's demand between them. Basis: City Journal $2,500 to $750 example; Anenberg and Kung on variety; Auerbach preferential attachment.
27. **Turf reputation.** A unit that holds the same pitch for a long time earns a "claimed" flag; rival AI trucks avoid it, and a player who parks there risks a dispute event (lost day, minor damage). Ice cream territory runs as franchise zones with rival "imposter" trucks. Basis: Fordham study [unconfirmed]; Mister Softee turf cases.
28. **Brick-and-mortar pushback.** Parking a truck within one block of a player-owned or AI restaurant of the same category lowers that restaurant's lunch traffic, and repeated presence can trigger a BID complaint that adds a temporary restricted street. Basis: the 2011 W 51st/52nd complaints.
29. **Jingle rule.** Ice cream trucks get a demand bonus while driving through residential streets with the jingle on, and a noise fine if they play it while parked. Basis: NYC noise code.
