# NYC food trucks and street food vending

Dated background, researched 7 October 2026. This is real-world inspiration for the mod's design, not a spec and not current legal advice. The rules and prices were a snapshot on that date and may have changed. The game's own choices are in [the design](../design.md).

Every fact carries a source link. Tags:

- [unconfirmed]: from a single blog, forum, student project or vendor marketing page, or a page read only through a search snippet.
- [inference]: my own reasoning or arithmetic, not a sourced fact.

Dollar figures are nominal for the year given. Many of the best operating numbers are old (2011 to 2019), because the city has not published a post-pandemic vendor survey ([IBO testimony, May 2025](https://www.ibo.nyc.gov/assets/ibo/downloads/pdf/economy-and-employment/2025/2025-may-street-vendor-testimony-to-the-nyc-council.pdf)).

## Person licence vs unit permit

- The mobile food vendor licence belongs to a person. It is uncapped, one per person, and needs the Food Protection Course for Mobile Vendors ([DOHMH](https://www.nyc.gov/health/mobilefood)). It costs $50 for two years and the course $53. Honourably discharged veterans pay nothing for the licence ([NYC Business](https://nyc-business.nyc.gov/nycbusiness/description/mobile-food-vending-unit-permit-full-term/apply)).
- The mobile food vending permit belongs to a specific cart or truck, and the law caps how many exist ([DOHMH](https://www.nyc.gov/health/mobilefood)). Several licensed people can work one permitted unit ([Auerbach 2024](https://jauerbach.github.io/research/auerbach_vendor_2024.pdf)). The permit need not be in the vendor's name ([DSNY vendor guide](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dsny/downloads/what-we-do/cleaning/street-vending-enforcement/mobile-food-vendor-educational-highlights-tips.pdf)).
- The cap was 5,100 permits in 2025: 2,800 citywide full-term, 200 borough-specific, 100 veteran or disabled, 1,000 seasonal (1 April to 31 October) and 1,000 green carts ([NY1, May 2025](https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/politics/2025/05/07/city-councilmembers-consider-legislation-to-lift-cap-on-vendor-licenses)). A January 2026 law package adds permits from July 2026 ([NYC Council](https://council.nyc.gov/press/2026/01/29/3066/)), so the cap figure is a dated snapshot.
- A full-term permit with on-site food prep cost $200 for two years initially and $438 to renew ([NYC Business](https://nyc-business.nyc.gov/nycbusiness/description/mobile-food-vending-unit-permit-full-term/apply)). Applicants also need workers' compensation and disability insurance, a sales tax certificate and no outstanding Health Code fines (same source).
- Waitlists for food permits and general vendor licences together exceeded 20,000 people in 2025 ([IBO, May 2025](https://www.ibo.nyc.gov/assets/ibo/downloads/pdf/economy-and-employment/2025/2025-may-street-vendor-testimony-to-the-nyc-council.pdf)).

## Inspection and commissary

- Carts and trucks have had letter grades since December 2018. A is 0 to 13 violation points, B 14 to 27, C 28 or more ([DOHMH vendor guide](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/doh/downloads/pdf/rii/mfv-what-vendors-need-to-know.pdf)).
- Inspection frequency follows the last score: an A means the next inspection in about a year, 14 to 27 points in 5 to 7 months, 28 or more in 3 to 5 months. A score of 14 or more triggers a reinspection within weeks (same source).
- Each unit carries a city location device so inspectors can find it. DOHMH queries it only when an inspection is due (same source).
- Every unit must be stored, cleaned and serviced overnight at an approved commissary or depot and must return daily. Units selling only whole produce or non-hazardous packaged food can use other facilities ([DOHMH](https://www.nyc.gov/health/mobilefood)).
- All food must come from a commissary or another licensed source ([NYC Business](https://nyc-business.nyc.gov/nycbusiness/description/mobile-food-vending-unit-permit-full-term/apply)). Commissary capacity is limited, and DOHMH has said more vending needs more commissary space ([IBO, May 2025](https://www.ibo.nyc.gov/assets/ibo/downloads/pdf/economy-and-employment/2025/2025-may-street-vendor-testimony-to-the-nyc-council.pdf)).

## Legal siting

From the city's vendor guide ([DSNY and DOHMH guide, May 2023](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/dsny/downloads/what-we-do/cleaning/street-vending-enforcement/mobile-food-vendor-educational-highlights-tips.pdf)):

- No vending within 10 ft of a crosswalk at an intersection, a subway entrance or exit, or a driveway.
- Carts stand at the curb, at least 20 ft from any building entrance or exit, leaving a 12 ft clear sidewalk path.
- No vending in a bus stop, taxi stand or hospital no-standing zone.
- A truck in the roadway obeys all parking rules and may not vend from a metered parking space (34 RCNY 4-08(h)(7)).

The meter rule dates to 1965. After a 2011 ruling that food counts as merchandise, police pushed trucks out of Midtown. Food Truck Association members reported sales falls of 20% to 70%, and moving could take up to two hours and miss the lunch peak ([DNAinfo, Aug 2011](https://www.dnainfo.com/new-york/20110819/murray-hill-gramercy/food-truck-crackdown-spreads-midtown-east)). Carts on the sidewalk escape the rule.

DOHMH publishes street segments closed to food vending on set days and hours ([DOHMH restricted streets guide](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/doh/downloads/pdf/permit/mfv_restricted_streets.pdf)). I counted 190 segments, 131 of them in Manhattan. For example, Sixth Avenue from W 33rd to W 59th is closed Monday to Saturday 8 a.m. to 7 p.m. [inference] The restrictions bite hardest where weekday lunch demand is highest, so legal vending clusters on the cross streets that are not on the list.

Vending inside a park needs a Parks concession, auctioned for five years. In 2011 minimum bids for Central Park cart spots ran from $7,350 to $176,925 a year ([DNAinfo, 2011](https://www.dnainfo.com/new-york/20110405/manhattan/hot-dog-vendors-pay-top-dollar-central-park)).

## Demand

- Lunch: Yumpling's line formed from 11 a.m., and the window ran 11:30 a.m. to 2:30 p.m. ([CNBC, 2019](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/09/11/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-new-york-city-food-truck-owner.html)).
- Midtown office blocks such as W 49th between Sixth and Seventh fill with trucks by 7 a.m. (same source). Late-night carts such as the original Halal Guys at 53rd and Sixth run from about 8 p.m. to 4 a.m. ([SBS Food](https://www.sbs.com.au/food/article/global-roaming-new-yorks-supper-club/9643xaena)).
- Vendors in large US cities work three locations on a typical weekday and spend about six hours at the main one. 70% choose locations for a critical mass of people ([Institute for Justice, Upwardly Mobile](https://ij.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/10/upwardly-mobile-web-final.pdf)). This is US-wide, not NYC.
- Yumpling served 200 to 250 customers in a three-hour lunch with six staff ([CNBC, 2019](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/09/12/the-biggest-misconception-of-running-a-food-truck.html)). [inference] That is 67 to 83 customers an hour, or 11 to 14 per staff-hour.
- Weather: most NYC food vendors earn $250 to $1,000 a week in both summer and winter, with more under $250 in winter ([IRI survey report](https://immresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/Street-Vendor-Survey-Final.pdf)). US anecdotes put rainy lunches at half and December to February at 50% or more down ([search summary of local news](https://spectrumnews1.com/oh/columbus/news/2026/04/04/weather-bites-food-truck-sales); [Texas Monthly](https://www.texasmonthly.com/food/summer-heat-taco-truck-sales-slump/)) [unconfirmed, not NYC].
- Events: many operators make 30% or more of revenue from private events ([QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/growth/12-mobile-food-trends-future)).

- Halal cart, 2024: on a $3,000 day the Lower Manhattan co-owner keeps about $200 after food, supplies, propane, maintenance, insurance and wages ([NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2024/02/04/nyregion/nyc-costs-chicken-rice-cart.html)). [inference] A margin of about 7%.
- Chicken over rice at that cart was $10 in 2024, up from $6 before the pandemic (same source).
- A cart that did $2,500 a day did $750 a day across two carts after competitors arrived. Marginal-area vendors make about $100 a day. Hired cart workers got about $20 an hour ([City Journal, 2015](https://www.city-journal.org/html/street-food-fight-13741.html)).
- IBO puts average annual profit for mobile food vendors at about $46,000 (2019 survey, 2023 dollars). The original January 2024 figure of about $9,000 was corrected in April 2025 ([IBO, May 2025](https://www.ibo.nyc.gov/assets/ibo/downloads/pdf/economy-and-employment/2025/2025-may-street-vendor-testimony-to-the-nyc-council.pdf)).
- 62% of permitted food vendors clear $500 a week in summer, against 43% of unpermitted ones ([IRI](https://immresearch.org/wp-content/uploads/Street-Vendor-Survey-Final.pdf)).
- US averages, for scale only: net margin 6% to 9%, food cost 25% to 30% ([MenuTiger](https://www.menutiger.com/blog/average-food-truck-income)) [unconfirmed, vendor blog].
- Carts typically run with one or two people [inference from cart size; no source counts this]. Yumpling's truck ran six at lunch ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2019/09/11/a-day-in-the-life-of-a-new-york-city-food-truck-owner.html)).
