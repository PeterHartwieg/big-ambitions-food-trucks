# Balance calculator

Runs the mod's own Core demand model over the city to price a cart day. The findings are in [docs/balance/cart-economics.md](../../docs/balance/cart-economics.md). Nothing here ships with the mod: `tools/build-mod.sh` packages only `unity/FoodTrucks`, and this project is not in `FoodTrucks.sln`.

Revenue and profit here are earned on the day sold, for player and crew alike. In game, all window takings are paid at midnight in one transaction per vehicle/day (17 October 2026); this calculator does not model the bank balance or funds available for restocking. The payment timing changes no sales, cost or profit calculation here.

## Run it

```sh
cd tools/balance
dotnet build
dotnet bin/Debug/net10.0/balance.dll                          # today's data, price at the ceiling
dotnet bin/Debug/net10.0/balance.dll --pricing default         # today's data, default price
dotnet bin/Debug/net10.0/balance.dll --reports ingame          # the in-game case: two offices and a nightclub
```

It prints Markdown tables. `--out FILE` also writes them to a file. A full run takes about five minutes (the `compare` report is most of it); `--reports archetypes,costs` is instant.

| Option | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `--reports LIST` | Any of `archetypes,spots,single,routes,compare,pricing,costs,ingame,premium,pitches,foods,storage,pass,checks,crew-economics,crew-economics-checks` (default: `archetypes,spots,single,compare,pricing,costs`). `premium` lists each district's best pitches, the candidates for premium spots; `pitches` prices `data/pitches.json`: each premium spot's day with and without the spot held, and each event's hours with a booked place against the same hours without the event, less the fee. `compare` is staying all day against the best route over the same 16 hours, per district, and ends with the ladder table; `pricing` is the price sensitivity; `routes` the older fixed lunch/evening routes; `ingame` the spot found in game on 8 October (two offices at 20 and 40 m, a nightclub at 30 m) under the old share model and the additive model (the old-model column uses today's data, so it differs from the study's 0.3.0 column, which used 0.3.0's data), a pure office block, and customers per hour at every real pitch (p25, median, p90 per district). `foods` is the new-foods study ([docs/balance/new-foods.md](../../docs/balance/new-foods.md)): every cart's best day city-wide on a weekday and Saturday in May and July, a weekday in January and at the default price, its cost to start, its best summer park day and its rate at a few pure spots. It needs `--patch new-foods-proposal.json` for the new carts and takes about 20 minutes for all of them. `checks` runs the calculator's own regression checks and throws on a failure (a route must judge the stock a stay ended with, after any restock inside it; about 3 seconds). `storage` is the storage-per-piece study ([docs/balance/storage-per-piece.md](../../docs/balance/storage-per-piece.md)): carts and trucks with and without Extra Storage at their best spot, when the first item runs out, restocks, profit, and the cabinet's payback; with `--patch new-foods-proposal.json` for the grill rows, about 30 seconds. `pass` is the balance pass of 14 October 2026 ([docs/balance/balance-pass-2026-10-14.md](../../docs/balance/balance-pass-2026-10-14.md)): carts and trucks played and crewed at the poor, typical, good and best pitch and at premium spots, a day and a week with rent, wages and the delivery fee; how many vehicles the weekly phone limit (one for the fleet) supplies; the fee against hauling; parks. Stock loads use Core's `ProductInfo.StockWeight` (coffee and tea 0.25, pastries 0.5, other shipped products 1). Storage is per piece, as in Core's `Storage`: each menu item is held by the one piece that serves it (`EquipmentForProduct`), each piece holds the vehicle's `unitsPerPiece` (doubled with Extra Storage, `Cart(storage: true)`), and a piece serving several items is filled in the proportions the spot sells them. A stay restocks when the first item that had stock runs out (10 October 2026; before, when everything had); a restock keeps the stock on board and fills each piece's free room, and when there is no time for a trip the other pieces sell on until closing. A route goes straight on only while every piece holds at least half its room. Area appetite is applied once by Core's `DemandModel`, including mixed-area spots. The calculator has no separate park appetite or stock-weight rules. The archetype park pitch has the park presence a spot inside one of the game's park areas gets (about 0.70); a waterfront archetype sits beside it.

Who serves (14 October 2026): `Cart.Plan` keeps `CartDemand.Plan`'s capacity for the vehicle's whole staff (economy.json), and selling uses the people at the window as `CartSelling` does: capacity per server × team speed × servers (`Cart(servers, speed)`, default the vehicle's staff at speed 1). A player alone at a truck is one server. Trucks restock by driving (`--drive-speed`), carts by pushing. `RestockMode.None` is a crew that loads in its commissary's bay and sells until everything is out (Core `CrewRestock` loads only in the bay); a `Planner` with `Depots` restocks at the commissaries instead of the wholesalers.

Like `CartDemand.Plan` and `CartSelling.ServeCustomer`, each category offers its stocked item with the highest price conversion, breaking ties by more stock, then ordinal item name. Its accepted weight is the category share times that conversion. The plan carries these `SaleItems` and the `AddOnItem` into selling. The add-on is the best-converting item across add-on categories, with equal conversions keeping the first category encountered in stock; its conversion scales drinks per food buyer.

## Verify shipped balance

Headlines on the additive crowd model with the fourth pass's data (8 October 2026; dry weekday in May, market demand 100, wholesale restocking, the 16-hour day), from `--reports compare`: hot dog grill + cooler $1,975 best route; coffee machine + display case $2,393; truck stand-in $4,123. `--reports ingame` gives the in-game case and the typical-spot table. See section 6 of the study. With storage per piece (10 October 2026, after the first review round's fixes) the same run gives $2,058, $2,202 and $4,130 ([storage-per-piece.md](../../docs/balance/storage-per-piece.md)).

## Files

- `CrewEconomics.cs`, `crew-economics.json`: isolated weekly crew-route simulator, versioned illustrative assumptions and regression checks (instructions below).
- `CartModel.cs`: one cart's sales. `Cart.Plan` is `CartDemand.Plan` from `unity/FoodTrucks/Scripts/CartDemand.cs` with the game reads replaced by inputs; it calls Core's `DemandModel`, `PriceCurve`, `ServiceRate` and `SalesRate` unchanged.
- `City.cs`: spots, the classifier and walking times. `Days.cs`: single-spot days and routes.
- `city.json`: the city, from `make_city.py`. Rebuild it with `python3 make_city.py [--save SAVE.hsg]` (needs `~/big-copilot`). The committed file holds the fixed AI businesses and one late-game save's rival businesses.
- `game-prices.json`: item prices, wholesale prices, neighbourhood price indices and equipment prices, from Big Copilot.
- `new-foods-proposal.json`: the proposed data for the eight new foods (`--patch`); see the new-foods study.
- `proposed.json`: the third pass's patch, kept for reference. The fourth pass edited `data/demand.json` directly; applying this patch brings back third-pass values, so run without it.
- `store/`: the small-store yardstick. `extract_payload.py SAVE OUT.json` has Big Copilot read a save (read-only); `node store/store_yardstick.cjs OUT.json` runs Big Copilot's validated Open a store model on it at the default difficulty. Keep the extracted payload out of git: it comes from a personal save.

## Crew-route profitability sensitivity

`crew-economics` is a separate offline study; it changes no shipped runtime or data and uses no native rig/game installation. Run from the repository root:

```sh
dotnet build tools/balance/Balance.csproj
mkdir -p build/crew-economics
dotnet tools/balance/bin/Debug/net10.0/balance.dll \
  --reports crew-economics-checks --pricing default \
  --out build/crew-economics/checks.md

dotnet tools/balance/bin/Debug/net10.0/balance.dll \
  --reports crew-economics,crew-economics-checks --pricing default \
  --out build/crew-economics/crew-study.md \
  --json-out build/crew-economics/crew-study.json
```

Use `--crew-economics-config PATH` for a complete alternative version-2 assumptions JSON (default: [crew-economics.json](crew-economics.json)). Fields, units, ranges, finite values and schema are validated; unknown/missing fields are errors. Keep its explicit units string. Other reports cannot be combined with the crew reports, preventing conflicting `--json-out` formats. `crew-economics-checks` throws on regression failure and can run alone or with the study. Default inputs complete promptly; no external data or random traffic sampling is required.

The study honours `--pricing`, `--price-factor`, `--crew-skill`, `--crew-satisfaction`, `--market`, `--service`, `--data`, `--city`, `--prices`, `--patch`, `--deliveries-per-week` and `--fee-sharing`. Its menu, two employees, 07:00–19:00 paid shifts and fixed service windows are declared study policy; `--carts`, pushing/restock and `--drive-speed` options belong to the older reports and do not configure this study. The existing CLI defaults to **ceiling pricing**; the commands above deliberately request **default item prices**, without claiming ceiling is the player's default. A patch changes only in-memory inputs; the initial study uses current unpatched data.

`CrewEconomics.cs` reuses `Cart`, `Selling`, current `RuleTables`, stock weights, per-piece capacities, crew service rates and weekday demand. Routes use actual building anchors in two commissary districts at baseline demand ranks p25/p50/p90. Each has three purposeful bounded stops: total straight-line distance including depot travel must lie in the configured short/local `[0,700)` m or long `[700,1600)` m band. After the demand-ranked first pitch, choose distinct downstream pitches with the largest summed Wednesday nominal-window contribution (revenue minus sold COGS) for lunch and afternoon. Each selection score starts with the same full target, so it ignores inter-stop stock carryover and travel; the actual route simulation includes both. Ties use shorter total distance, then ordinal second/third address. Missing bands are reported. These are illustrative biased selections, not representative player routes or optimized finite-stock itineraries. Selection and the matched one-pitch alternative (best **among that route's own pitches under legacy only**, not globally optimal) are frozen before scenario comparisons. The Markdown and JSON emit selection rules, exact input coordinates, windows and each leg's distance. All pitch and commissary coordinates use `city.json`'s approximate **regional map frame in metres**, not native world coordinates. Explicit native bay positions from `commissaries.json` are excluded from distance calculations because their frame differs; the depot uses its building's map anchor, with unknown bay offset. Map anchors and straight-line distances multiplied by the explicit road factor are **not measured road geometry or certified parking**. Prior map-scale work suggests distances may be off 15–35%; this is not a measured-route confidence interval.

Every case runs the explicitly dated illustrative dry May week 2026-05-04 (Monday) to 2026-05-10 (Sunday). Loading defaults to 30 game minutes, matching current Core restock; all cases use the same configurable duration. The truck starts empty at its depot; morning loading tops up to the same finite per-piece targets, preserving leftover stock. Food never refills on the road. Sufficient commissary stock before 07:00 is assumed independently of deliveries' actual arrival or funding. Stock, position and fuel carry through the week. Dispatch reserves homeward travel within the shift before service; delays trim service and can skip stops. Skipped-stop home completions show reduced operation and an infeasible original schedule explicitly. A failed home return or wholly unserviceable day yields null ordinary weekly profit, with daily failure reasons and actual incurred costs retained in an always-numeric `realizedOperatingBalance`. That balance is revenue minus all modelled incurred operating expenses, including fixed costs on zero-sales days; it does not claim unfinished travel has been completed. Fixed shift wages remain equal; extra wages are zero.

The precise baseline is **legacy travel timing under the proposed fixed-shift/return-reserve policy**, not current shipping business outcomes. It calls **Core.Fleet.Travel.Minutes** with current shipped travel rules and no fuel in the same simulator/policy as every physical case. Fixed shift hours differ from the `pass` report's Core AutoSchedule payroll; those profits should not be compared directly. Adjacent windows have zero transfer slack, so moving consumes part of the next window; baseline negative transition slack is emitted. There is no traffic-adaptive retiming. Losses can overstate what a player adapting the route would experience; only the continuous 08–18 matched stay models adaptation. Normal/heavy physical cases use illustrative **8/4 m/s**, road factor **1.3**, and **3/10 game minutes of waits per leg**. One real second is one game minute, so physical metres/second are metres/game minute: no factor 60. No representative native timing or fuel calibration exists; **the 1 m/s, 31 m native probe is never extrapolated**. These are sensitivity assumptions, not measured Persistent City traffic distributions. The code-based 1:1 clock assumption is not validated here against native wall time. Mild 3/10 game-minute waits mean 3/10 real seconds under that assumption. Station filling at 15 game minutes likewise means 15 real seconds and remains explicitly hypothetical. The report adds an exact zero-wait **fuel-disabled** physical control at `roadFactor / legacyDriveMinutesPerMetre / legacyRoadFactor` (32.5 m/s with current inputs), which must exactly match legacy timing, economics and stock; it is mathematical time compression, not an attainable truck setting.

Physical cases use an 80 L tank, 2 L/km, idle consumption **0.02 L/game minute during lights/parking waits only**, an 8 L reserve and an assumed **$1.30/L reference**, not a verified current modpack price. Ordinary cases start full; paired normal/heavy stress cases start at 10 L. A morning full-day fuel forecast plus reserve triggers at most one synthetic station loop (400 straight metres each way, physical travel/fuel, 15 game minutes filling). The truck must reach it with existing fuel, buys actual empty capacity on arrival, and returns to its depot. Insufficient reachability, tank capacity or shift time is explicit infeasible. Engines are off during loading, selling and filling; there is no free recovery or real station-access/reservation claim.

Profit is **pre-tax operating profit**, charging sold-stock COGS once, equal fixed wages, current commissary rent, delivery allocation and **fuel consumed including the value used from the starting tank**. Fuel purchased/cash is reported separately and never charged again. Capex/depreciation, financing, permits/licences, damage, maintenance, fines, spoilage and health/regulars/competition feedback are excluded. Open minutes include time after stock depletion; sales minutes count stocked `Selling.Run` simulation steps, not cashier busy time. Missed peak demand is unconstrained full-menu demand evaluated in the actual closed intervals (lunch 11:30–14:30), not guaranteed lost sales.

A bounded crossed speed grid (1/2/4/6/8/12/16/25 m/s) and fuel grid (0.5/2/6 L/km), with normal waits held constant, runs on the long median-demand route in each configured district. The 1/2 m/s points are diagnostic probe-test limits, not production targets or evidence of economic acceptability. Grid points 16/25 m/s and the exact-equivalence control are mathematical time-compression controls, not attainable truck settings. Each speed is reported as game minutes/straight km excluding waits/ceiling. A separate hypothetical wait grid of 0/30/90/180 game minutes per nonzero leg runs on the same subset at normal speed/fuel, including station legs and their idle fuel. Total station-loop time and its raw overlap with 08–18 are emitted separately; overlap is not a causal lost-sales estimate or a second expense. Sampled loss/preference sets are labelled discrete; no monotonic break-even search is used. JSON contains numeric per-day and per-week economics, stock boundaries, sale intervals, trips, refuels, all assumptions and SHA256 hashes of the actual input files and calculator/Core sources. The self-contained checks exercise timer/time units, actual-time demand, finite stock, fixed shifts, return reservation, fuel conservation and fills, unreachable fuel/shift outcomes, missed windows, repeatability and frozen single-pitch selection.

The study excludes native road-graph routing, parking/access failures, stochastic traffic/jams, regulars/reputation, and nightlife service after 18:00. Results are conditional tuning evidence, not a live-traffic-calibrated commercial-viability prediction. Schema 2 adds purposeful distance bands, wait/control scenarios, explicit dates and incurred-loss reporting. Preserve schema-1 first-run evidence separately.

Reporting distinguishes stock exhaustion and schedule completion: daily `soldOutItems` requires positive opening/loaded stock, actual positive sales and ending stock at or below 1e-6; weekly `soldOutItemDays` counts those days per item and appears in the selling-time table. Empty unserved items do not count. The comparison section reports computed legacy M/S win counts (staying already wins 10/12 in both frozen-input pricing studies) and legacy sold-out item-day totals for both alternatives. This is a stock-constrained comparison with no midday restock. The stay advantage cannot all be attributed to traffic; no unlimited-stock counterfactual quantifies stock's causal share. Comparative and sensitivity rows label full completion, reduced operation with skipped-stop counts, and infeasibility separately; discrete thresholds list M/S reduced-operation speed sets independently of infeasible points.
